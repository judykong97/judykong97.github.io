*This article introduces **Judy's Homemade Asian Fusion Turkey Recipe**, after nearly a decade of Friendsgiving turkey iterations. The recipe blends traditional turkey-making with Asian culinary inspirations, including but not limited to:*

* Nanjing Roasted Duck (from my hometown)
* Cantonese Char Siu (BBQ Pork)
* Thai Grilled Pork Jowl

It adds a sweet, fruity depth to the traditional turkey flavor. Inspired by the famous “Three-Way Peking Duck” (一鸭三吃), this recipe also includes side dishes: turkey soup and turkey noodles.

---

### 📝 Recipe

> *Note: Like many traditional Chinese recipes, this one does not follow exact measurements. Seasoning = "just right." Adjust turkey size and baking time based on other sources.*

---

### 🦃 Day BEFORE Thanksgiving

#### Ingredients
- Fresh turkey (from the refrigerated section!! NOT frozen)
- Stuffing breadcrumbs
- Onions × 2
- A bunch of celery
- Oranges × 2
- Lemons and limes × 4-5
- (Optional) Noodles (for turkey noodle soup)

#### Seasonings
- Salt (lots)
- Black pepper (lots)
- Butter
- Herbs (rosemary REQUIRED, parsley/sage/thyme optional)
- Honey (plenty – at least one small jar)
- Turkey gravy
- Cranberry sauce (preferably the fresh one from Whole Foods hot bar)

#### Kitchen Tools
- 2 disposable roasting trays
- 1 empty wine bottle (straight cylinder preferred)
- Basting brush (or spoon)
- Lots of foil
- Large cutting board / tray (to support the turkey)
- Knife, pan, basic cookware
- (Optional) Large soup pot

---

#### Procedure

**Step 0**: Unpack turkey, remove giblets and neck, pat dry  
**Step 1**: Cut 1 onion into chunks, rub inside and outside of turkey  
**Step 2**: Massage turkey thoroughly with salt and black pepper; let sit 2 hrs  
**Step 3**: Apply butter massage  
**Step 4**: Stand turkey up on the wine bottle (or hang dry if possible)  
**Step 5**: Let sit overnight. Cover in foil and refrigerate if room is warm

---

### 🔥 Thanksgiving Day

#### 10AM–12PM: Stuffing & prep
**Steps 6–9**:  
Chop celery/onions, sauté with butter and salt, add breadcrumbs & herbs

**Step 10**: Transfer turkey from wine bottle to clean roasting tray  
**Step 11**: Stuff with the mixture, plug with a whole orange

---

#### 12PM–6:30PM: Roasting

**Step 12**: Wrap wings and drumsticks in foil to prevent burning  
**Step 13**: Wrap entire tray in foil to avoid charring the skin  
**Step 14**: Preheat oven to 400°F. Roast for 30–60 mins (to seal moisture)  
**Step 15**: Take turkey out, generously apply honey all over  
**Step 16**: Re-wrap, lower oven to 325°F, roast for 2 hrs  
**Step 17**: Take out, apply honey again  
**Step 18**: Re-wrap, continue 325°F for another 2 hrs  
**Step 19**: Take out, apply honey one last time  
**Step 20**: Remove all foil, return turkey to oven at 400°F for color  
**Step 21**: Roast another 30–60 mins as needed (check internal temp, or ensure no red juice leaks)  
**Step 22**: Decorate! Remove old orange, replace with fresh one, add rosemary/lemon/cranberry on top  
**Step 23**: Serve & celebrate!

### 💤 The Day AFTER Thanksgiving

By the time Thanksgiving is over, you've had *enough* turkey. So the legendary “Three-Way Turkey” dishes (aka: leftover hacks) are reserved for post-midnight cravings (when you're online shopping at 3AM and your stomach starts making decisions), or for lunch the next day.

Here's the move:  
Take the entire turkey frame + any leftover meat → toss it into a large soup pot.  
No seasoning needed. Just add water.  
Boil on high heat, then reduce to low and simmer for 30–60 minutes (or longer).  
**Voilà — turkey soup is born.**

If you're really hungry, throw in some noodles once the soup is ready.  
Just boil like you normally would.  
Tested: instant ramen and udon both work *surprisingly* well.

### 🔬 Highlights & Explanation

The repeated honey glazing every two hours is the game changer.  
It mimics the Nanjing roasted duck method of brushing sugar water repeatedly and reflects Thai BBQ's sweet-savory balance.  
Slow roasting with honey = juicy, flavorful, golden-skinned turkey.

### 🦆 Closing Remarks & Cultural Context

I grew up in Nanjing, where duck reigns supreme (joke goes: “no duck can swim across the Yangtze — they all get eaten”).  
No one roasts duck at home there — you just walk to the nearest shop.  
But living far from home, craving duck, no stores around... you DIY.  
This recipe came from my early college days when I confused the steps for turkey and roasted duck, applied honey every time we opened the oven — and it turned out amazing.
